Week 1 follows sunlight on its way into a solar cell, and counts what is lost at every step.

The Sun is a very hot ball of gas, and hot things glow: it sends out <Term id="thermal-radiation" /> spread over many colours, the <Term id="solar-spectrum" />. A solar cell doesn’t really care about watts. It counts <Term id="photon">photons</Term>, because every photon it absorbs frees **one** electron, as long as the photon carries at least the cell’s <Term id="bandgap" />.

Before a photon can free an electron, two things have to go right. It has to **get in**: part of the light bounces off the surface (about a third for bare silicon!), and that is pure optics. Then it has to **be absorbed** inside the cell before it leaks out the back, which depends on the colour of the light and the thickness of the cell.

So this week is one chain: *how much light arrives → how many photons is that → how many get in → how many are absorbed*. Every percent lost along the chain is a percent of current the cell will never deliver.
