## Protons, electrons and the atomic number

An atom is a small positive nucleus surrounded by electrons. The <Term id="atomic-number" /> $Z$ is the number of protons in the nucleus. A **neutral** atom has exactly $Z$ electrons, so its total charge is zero. Silicon has $Z = 14$, boron 5, phosphorus 15.

## Shells: only the outer one matters

Electrons fill shells from the inside out. The first shell holds 2 electrons, the second 8. In the notation of lecture 2 (slide 15), silicon is

$$
1s^2\;2s^2 2p^6\;3s^2 3p^2
$$

That is 2 + 8 closed-shell electrons, plus **4** in the third shell. The outer electrons are the <Term id="valence-electron">valence electrons</Term>, the only ones that take part in chemical bonds. A full outer shell would hold 8, so silicon is "half full".

## Four bonds per silicon atom

Silicon shares each of its 4 valence electrons with a neighbour, and each neighbour shares one back: four <Term id="covalent-bond">covalent bonds</Term> of two electrons each. Now every atom "sees" 8 electrons around it. In crystalline silicon every atom has exactly 4 neighbours at equal distances and bond angles of 109.5°, repeated in a regular pattern.

**Breaking a bond** (with heat or a photon) frees one electron and leaves an empty spot: that is the electron–hole pair of week 2.

## Why boron and phosphorus

Elements in the same column of the periodic table have the same number of valence electrons:

| Column | Valence electrons | Examples | In a Si crystal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 13 (III) | 3 | B, Ga, In | one bond incomplete → accepts an electron (**acceptor**) |
| 14 (IV) | 4 | C, Si, Ge | fits in perfectly, no effect on carriers |
| 15 (V) | 5 | P, As | one electron left over → donates it (**donor**) |

<KeyIdea>
Replacing a Si atom by P or B never charges the crystal: an extra (or missing) electron comes with an extra (or missing) proton. Only the *mobility* of that one electron changes.
</KeyIdea>

## Counting atoms in a crystal

A crystal is a <Term id="unit-cell" /> stacked in three directions. Atoms on the boundary are shared with neighbouring cells: a corner atom counts for 1/8, a face atom for 1/2, an atom inside for 1. The silicon (diamond) cell has 8 corners, 6 faces and 4 atoms inside:

$$
8 \times \tfrac18 + 6 \times \tfrac12 + 4 \times 1 = 8 \text{ atoms per cell}
$$

With the cell edge $a = 5.43 \times 10^{-10}$ m $= 5.43 \times 10^{-8}$ cm, one cell has volume $a^3 = 1.60 \times 10^{-22}$ cm³, so silicon contains $8/a^3 = 5.0 \times 10^{22}$ atoms per cm³ (instruction 2 Q1b). Convert to cm **before** cubing.
